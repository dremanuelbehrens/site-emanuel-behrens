About
Medicine is not just technique and knowledge. It is daily effort, empathy, humanity and constant learning.

Hello! My name is Emanuel and I was born in Curitiba, Brazil. I am a mechanical technician from IFPR, a physician from UFSC, and I hold a postgraduate degree in Urgent and Emergency Medicine from Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein.

Today I am a public servant at the UFSC School of Medicine in Araranguá, where I work as technical-administrative staff (TAE), Professor of Internal Medicine and Clinical Preceptor in Internal Medicine and Urgent and Emergency Care.

My professional dream has always been to teach. I love to study, I love to teach, and I believe that by teaching Medicine and helping to train good professionals I can reach many people. Education has a ripple effect: if I can positively influence one student, and that student in turn influences other professionals or cares for people in an ethical, humane and correct way every day, my work will be done.

I study. Every day.
Even after graduating and with an established career, learning cannot stop. That is how I stay up to date, reduce the chance of errors and commit to doing good work.

But without a doubt, the title I love most is father. It is what inspires me, every day, to try to be a better professional.

Emanuel Behrens

Frequently asked questions
Do you accept health insurance?

We only offer private in-person and telehealth appointments, but we issue an official invoice (Nota Fiscal) so you can request reimbursement from your health plan.

From what age?

Care is for patients aged 14 and over.

How long does an appointment last?

Appointments are never rushed: on average, 45 minutes to 1 hour, so there is time to listen to your story, examine you and explain things calmly.

Should I bring previous test results?

Yes, if you have them. Bring test results, prescriptions and a list of the medications you take — all of this helps with the diagnosis.

What payment methods do you accept?

Cash, PIX (Brazilian instant payment) and debit or credit card.

Where is the office?

Frequently asked questions
Is an online prescription accepted at pharmacies?

Yes. Prescriptions and test orders are issued with a digital signature and are valid throughout Brazil. Some controlled medications may require a paper prescription — in those cases, you will be told how to collect it.

Do you see patients from other cities?

Yes. Telehealth visits can be done from wherever you are.

How do I get the link for the visit?

The video call link is sent on WhatsApp before your scheduled time.

What if a physical examination is needed?

If a physical examination becomes essential at any point, you will be advised to book an in-person appointment at the office in Araranguá.

How do I pay?

By PIX or card, before the appointment. You will receive an official invoice (Nota Fiscal) to request reimbursement, if your plan offers it.

Telehealth provided in accordance with Brazilian Federal Council of Medicine (CFM) Resolution 2,314/2022. In case of severe pain, shortness of breath or other urgent symptoms, go to an emergency department.

For newly graduated physicians

The first months after graduation bring decisions that medical school does not teach: where to work, how to organize your routine, how to handle your first cases without the supervision you had before. In the mentorship, you have a physician by your side at this start, with a guidance meeting, a career management meeting and, if you wish, discussion of your clinical cases during your first month of practice.

Brazilian Law 15,378/2026

Patients' Rights Statute

Enacted in 2026, Brazil's Patients' Rights Statute sets out rights and duties that apply to all health care, public or private, in the country. Here, these rights are fully respected.

01Art. 12
Clear information

You have the right to complete, up-to-date and understandable information about your health, treatment options, risks and benefits.

02Arts. 6, 14 and 20
Consent and autonomy

You may accept or refuse procedures and withdraw consent at any time, appoint a representative and record your advance directives.

03Arts. 15, 16 and 19
Confidentiality and medical records

Information about your health is confidential. You decide who it is shared with, and you have access to your medical record, with a free copy.

04Art. 10
Dignity and respect

Care is provided without distinction based on race, religion, disability, origin, income or any other condition. You may be called by the name you prefer.

05Art. 18
Second opinion

You may seek another professional's opinion at any stage of care, with enough time to decide, except in emergencies.

06Art. 7
Companion

You have the right to a companion of your choice during appointments, unless their presence could compromise your health, privacy or safety.
